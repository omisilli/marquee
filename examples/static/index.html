marquee

A strip that scrolls round without a seam. The list is written once; the package copies it beside itself, as many times as the window needs, and moves it by one list's length, round and round. Mouse over one: it slows to a stop. Tab into one: it stops, so a link can be read. No dependencies.

The browser's own scroll With Lenis

1 · Flow — the announcement bar

Round and round, at a speed in px a second

--pg-marquee-speed is px a second, not a time: a longer list goes round at the same speed. --pg-marquee-hover: under the mouse it pauses, slows, or goes on. The button at the end stops it and goes on — anything with data-pg-marquee-toggle and aria-controls; moving for more than five seconds, a strip should have one.

2 · Step — one at a time

Each item comes, stays, and the next takes its place

--pg-marquee: step. An item stays --pg-marquee-hold, the move takes --pg-marquee-duration on --pg-marquee-easing. Up, each message the bar's own size; or left, side by side. The arrows are data-pg-marquee-prev and -next; under the mouse it waits.

4 · Columns — testimonials going up and down

Up or down: a column with a height of its own

--pg-marquee-direction: up or down, each column its own speed. Going up or down the window needs a height, the site's — here 28rem — and is best written with data-pg-marquee-axis="y" already, so it is a column from the first paint. Below 48rem, one column.

5 · Overflow — only when it does not fit

A long title moves, a short one stands still

--pg-marquee: overflow: while the list fits the window it stands still, with no copies (data-pg-marquee-fits); when it does not, it flows. Narrow the window: more titles start to move.

6 · With the page's scroll — two bands crossing

Faster as the page goes down, turning back as it goes up

--pg-marquee-scroll: the page's scroll speed, px a second, times this number, is added to the flow — scroll down hard and the bands race, scroll up and they turn back. --pg-marquee-turn: keep: having turned, they keep that way until the page goes down again. No GSAP: the package reads the scroll itself, Lenis or not. The bands are plain CSS, rotated.
